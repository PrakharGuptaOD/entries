The Genesis of Logic.

There is a distinct moment when technology shifts from being a tool you consume to a medium you control. For a long time, the systems governing the screens in front of me felt like magic—impenetrable black boxes that functioned on rules written by architects I would never meet. But eventually, the curiosity to dismantle those boxes outweighed the comfort of simply using them.

Writing those first few lines of code was less about creating something profound and more about establishing a dialogue with the machine. It was a crude, imperfect language at first. The syntax was unforgiving, and the errors were frequent. Yet, there was an undeniable clarity in the logic. If you provided the correct instructions, the machine obeyed without hesitation.

As I moved deeper into frameworks and architectures, the scale of what could be built expanded. It was no longer just about printing text to a console; it was about designing environments, automating responses, and structuring data in a way that mimicked human reasoning. This was the foundation—the quiet realization that logic could be weaponized to build entirely new worlds.
